# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be straight with people. Argue with the evidence, not the person.
**Entities are measured. Their creators are not.**

## In practice

**Expected**

- Disagree directly, with reasoning anyone can check.
- Accept a low score, or contest it with evidence. Both are respectable.
- Declare conflicts of interest before anyone has to ask.
- Say "I was wrong" when you were. It costs nothing here.

**Not accepted**

- Attacking a creator rather than a result.
- Pressuring an officer privately to change a score. Contest it in public or not at all.
- Submitting an entity with false disclosure or provenance. A low TRUST score is survivable;
  lying about it is not.
- Harassment, demeaning comments, or bringing someone's personal characteristics into a
  technical argument.
- Using this benchmark to rank or celebrate successful deception.

## Scope

Applies to issues, discussions, pull requests, and any space where you represent the project.

## Enforcement

Report to the maintainers, privately if the issue involves a maintainer. Reports are handled
confidentially.

Consequences scale with severity and repetition: a correction, a warning, removal of officer
status, or a ban. Officer removal is announced publicly with a reason — accountability runs
both directions.

## One specific note

Scores here can affect how people's work is perceived. That is a real responsibility.
It is a reason to be **careful and honest**, not a reason to be soft. An inflated score to
spare someone's feelings is a disservice to every other entity in the cohort.
