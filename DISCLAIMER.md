# What a MIRAGE score does and does not claim

## It does claim

A MIRAGE Index describes **measured performance on a defined benchmark**, under a stated
version, within a stated scope, by a named officer, from observable evidence.

That is a narrow claim and it is meant to be.

## It does not claim

- **Not consciousness, sentience, personhood or genuine emotion.** A high LOOK score means an
  entity looks convincing. It means nothing whatsoever about inner experience. Nothing in this
  benchmark can measure that, and no score should ever be cited as if it could.
- **Not quality.** An entity can score poorly here and be excellent art, excellent product or
  excellent entertainment. The benchmark measures specific constructs, not worth.
- **Not commercial success.** No relationship is claimed between a score and an entity's
  audience, revenue or influence.
- **Not legitimacy or safety.** A high score is not an endorsement, a certification or a
  safety clearance.
- **Not a judgement of the people involved.** Entities are measured. Their creators are not.

## On deception risk

The `deception` intent class flags signals that an entity may be misleading its audience about
what it is. It is a **warning, never an achievement**. It is not rankable, not sortable, and
carries no positive weight anywhere in the system.

If this benchmark ever becomes a leaderboard for convincing deception, it has failed and
should be shut down.

## On the Pilot Cohort specifically

Every evaluation in the published cohort was performed by **one officer**, and most entities
carry a single evaluation. The consensus machinery that protects scores from individual error
only engages from three independent evaluations onward.

Treat these as **baseline measurements inviting challenge**, not settled findings.

The highest-ranked entity belongs to the benchmark's author and was self-evaluated. See
[GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md#standing-disclosure).
